Sources: a walk-through of every signed-in screen, a full read of the source code, and the automated design scanner. I only looked at the form builder, not the published forms.

Surfaces, not boxes

Lists sit flat on one white sheet. A row lifts only when you point at it. No borders, no coloured top bars, no card inside a card.

Everything you press is round

Pill buttons, pill search, round icon buttons, a pill behind the active menu item. Square corners are kept for content, like files and photos.

Shapes that respond

Rings you can tap to filter, chips that show a tick when on, rows that reveal their actions on hover, and an Undo bar instead of a confirmation dialog.

Shape system: from boxes to pills

Most of the screen-by-screen notes come from these few decisions. Change them in src/theme/editorial.ts once, and most screens follow.

Corners

Today the app uses three square-ish corner sizes (5px, 8px, 12px) on everything, so a tag, a button and a page all read as "box". Proposed: one shape per kind of thing.

Now

badge 5 · control 8 · card 12

Proposed

anything you press: pill · new-request button: 18 · sheets: 24 · icon buttons and avatars: circle

Type

The code uses 51 different text sizes across 1,365 places, including half-pixel sizes and a mix of pixel and rem units. Proposed: six sizes. Figtree is optional. It has Google Sans's rounded feel, but staying on Inter is fine.

Buttons

Today nearly every action is a filled navy rectangle, so five "Open" buttons stacked down a page are all shouting. Proposed: four levels of emphasis, plus one "New request" button per screen.

Status and state

Today status is shown in up to four ways at once: a coloured top bar, a coloured icon tile, a coloured progress bar and an uppercase label. Proposed: a dot and a word in a pill. Hovering anything clickable gives it a light navy tint.

Before the app, and while it loads

index.html · LoadingScreen.tsx · LazyRoute.tsx

The first two things every person sees. Today it's a blank white page, then a screen showing six progress indicators at once.

  1. P2A blank white page until the app's code has downloaded
    The page starts completely empty, so on a slow connection people stare at white. Put the logo and a ring directly in index.html using plain CSS, so they show instantly and the app replaces them. Also: the browser bar colour is still the old Microsoft blue #0078D4 (should be navy #0F3D91), and the page has no description for link previews.
    index.html · <div id="root"></div> · meta theme-color
  2. P1Six progress indicators for one wait
    A spinner, a percentage, "Loading...", a sentence, a progress bar and a 5-step checklist all report the same thing. Keep one ring that fills, with one sentence under it that changes as it goes: "Signing you in", then "Getting your forms", then "Almost there".
    src/components/auth/LoadingScreen.tsx
  3. P2The steps describe the system, not the person
    "Token state", "PMW HR Docs site" and "HR Forms Owner and Form Builder Superuser access" mean nothing to an employee. Only show this detail if a step fails, and then say what to do: "We couldn't reach SharePoint. Try again, or contact IT at …".
    App.tsx loading steps
  4. P3The checklist runs off the bottom of the screen
    At 856px tall the last step sits below the fold, so a loading screen gets a scroll bar. Six things moving at once also makes the wait feel longer than one ring would. When the ring goes away, fade it out rather than cutting to the dashboard.
  5. P1Opening a tab for the first time wipes the whole screen
    Every page's loading state is the full-screen loader, so the first visit to any tab removes the header and menu and shows the boot splash again. It looks like the app restarted. Keep the menu in place and load inside the page area, with placeholder rows. Wait 200ms before showing anything, so pages that are already cached don't flash. Keep the full-screen loader for sign-in only.
    App.tsx:1697 withSubmissions · App.tsx:1766–2081 LazyRoute fallback · AdminGuard.tsx:73
  6. P2Leftovers from the old colour scheme
    The loader still draws blobs in the retired Microsoft blue and purple, wavy lines, and a glow that pulses forever even when the device asks for reduced motion. The 36px percentage is the loudest thing on the screen. Remove all of it.
    LoadingScreen.tsx:79–138, 152–167, 184–194
  7. P2After an update, old tabs show a generic error
    When a new version is deployed, a tab that was already open can fail to load the next page and lands on the generic error screen. Catch that case and show a pill banner: "A new version is ready · Reload".
    LazyRoute.tsx:77

Sign-in

/ · ChoiceScreen.tsx

The front door. It already looks calm, so most of the changes are wording and making the two ways in easier to tell apart.

  1. P1The product has seven names
    "PMW HR Group Portal" (browser tab), "PMW Group HR Portal" (this heading), "PMW HR Forms" (app header), "PMW HR Form" (inner tabs), "PMW Careers", "PMW International Berhad HR Forms" (form footer) and "PMW HR FORM" (the PDF stamp). Use whatever is printed on the QR posters, everywhere, and set it from one helper.
    index.html <title> · ChoiceScreen.tsx · document.title in AdminRoutingPage / AdminOrgPage / ApprovalDashboard
  2. P2"or" with nothing after it
    When Google sign-in isn't set up, the "or" divider and both Google sentences still show with no button to press. Hide the divider and the Google text when the Google button isn't there.
    ChoiceScreen.tsx ~238–293 · googleSignInConfigured()
  3. P3The Google rule is explained twice
    One paragraph in the middle and a footer below both say "staff use Microsoft, everyone else uses Google". Put the choice in the button labels ("I work at PMW" / "I'm a guest") and keep a single footnote.
  4. P3The logo is tiny and the card adds a box around nothing
    Remove the card. Put the logo on a round tinted backing at 64px, with the buttons on the page background. Two full-width pill buttons at equal size make both choices look equally valid. A global style also draws a thin outline around every image, which is why the logo has a box around it.
    index.css:72–76 img outline
  5. P2No hover feedback, and a focus ring you can't see
    The Microsoft button's hover colour is the same as its normal colour. The keyboard focus ring is pale blue on white, about 1.3:1 contrast, so keyboard users can't see where they are. Hover: a light tint over the button. Focus: a 2px navy ring.
    ChoiceScreen.tsx:367, 563
  6. P2The guest sign-in looks like a different company
    The guest screen uses a Georgia serif headline, a square black button, and an ink-bordered card with a yellow strip at a different corner size. Rebuild it from the same pills and type as everything else.
    GuestLanding.tsx:135–255

Navigation

AppShell.tsx · SectionTabs.tsx · navigation.ts

The source notes say the phone-style bottom bar at every width was a deliberate choice. Gmail answers the same question differently: a left menu on desktop, a bottom bar on phones, one set of menu items behind both. The working mockup above shows the desktop half.

  1. P2The header shows the section, not the page
    "Forms" appears as the title on My Submissions and All Submissions too. "Admin" covers seven different tools. Title the page itself, and show the section only through the highlighted menu item.
  2. P1Admin has more tabs than fit
    Form Builder, Routing, Organisation, Opportunities and Applications already cut off at 800px wide, and Cards, Learning and Guest members are hidden behind a small arrow. Group them: Forms (builder, routing, organisation), Careers (openings, applications, cards), People (guests, learning). Then 3 or 4 chips fit, and the overflow opens in a menu.
    src/config/navigation.ts
  3. P1A phone bar on a 1440px monitor, with 10px labels
    At every size the menu is a 60px bar along the bottom. Its labels are 10px, below the 11px minimum the scanner checks for, and the selected item is marked only by a 2px amber line above the icon. On desktop: a left menu like the one above, with a pill behind the selected item and counts of what's waiting. On phones: a bottom bar with a 52×28 pill behind the selected icon and 12px labels.
    AppShell.tsx:385–392 · fontSize 0.625rem · detector: undersized-ui-text ×6
  4. P3The account role text is too small to read
    "Administrator · Form Builder" is about 10.5px under your name. Move it into the account menu, as Gmail does: click the avatar to get a round card with your name, roles, Switch account and Sign out.
    AppShell.tsx:237 · 0.66rem
  5. P2Every page change throws away your place
    The page area is rebuilt and re-animated on every navigation, so going back to a long list resets the scroll position and filters. Fade between pages without rebuilding them, as Gmail does when you return to the inbox.
    AppShell.tsx:310–316 key={pathname}
  6. P3The skip link hides under the header, and there's no Help
    The "skip to content" link appears underneath the sticky header. The account menu has no "Help and contact HR" item.
    AppShell.tsx:117–139
  7. P3DESIGN.md still describes the sidebar
    The design notes describe a 224px navy sidebar at ≥1024px that the code no longer has. Update them in the same change that brings back a left menu, or the notes and the app will disagree a second time.
    DESIGN.md "Navigation"

Dashboard

/admin/dashboard · /user/dashboard · DashboardPage.tsx · StatsRow.tsx

This should answer "what needs me?" in one glance. Today the first screen is mostly a careers advert, and the one useful sentence tells you where to click instead of being a link.

  1. P1A careers advert takes the top half of the screen
    "Grow into your next role" fills about 40% of the first screen on an HR admin's dashboard, and its slide dots are 6px (the scanner also flags the dot's width animation). It also changes slide every 4.4 seconds with no way to pause, which fails WCAG 2.2.2. When an admin hasn't set any cards, it fills the space with stock wording. Shrink it to a one-line pill banner lower down: "3 openings this week · View".
    CareerPortalCarousel.tsx:137, 393 · DashboardPage.tsx:107
  2. P1"Needs your attention" isn't about you
    For an admin, "157 submissions still moving" counts everyone's submissions, not the ones waiting on this person. Approvers do their real work from email links, and the dashboard never shows "waiting on you". Start with a greeting, then a "Waiting on you" list linking to each review, then your recent submissions. Also check whether 157 pending and 0 approved is real data or a counting bug.
    DashboardPage.tsx:110 · bucketSubmissions
  3. P1An instruction where a link should be
    "Open Forms → My Submissions to see where each one has stopped" asks people to navigate there themselves. Make the whole line tappable and have it open the filtered list.
  4. P2Four boxes, each saying its number twice
    "157" over "157 visible submissions", "0" over "0 approved submissions". The Total progress bar is always full, so it carries no information. Each tile also has a coloured top bar and a coloured icon tile. Replace them with three rings you can tap; the total is just the sum.
    StatsRow.tsx
  5. P2"Pending" and "Total" are the same number
    When everything is pending, two tiles say 157 and two say 0. That's half the space spent on nothing. Rings make a gap like that easy to see (the amber ring nearly full, green almost empty), which is the actual story.
  6. P3A stray sentence pinned to the left edge
    "5 forms available to you · administrator access." has no left margin. On the sky background it measures 4.0:1 contrast, below the 4.5:1 minimum for text. Move it to Forms as a count.
    detector: low-contrast 4.0:1 #5a6880 on #bfddf4 · body-text-viewport-edge ×2
  7. P3Boxes inside boxes
    The scanner found 3 cards nested inside cards (the carousel sits in a white card with a coloured card inside it). Keep one level of surface.
    detector: nested-cards ×3
05

Forms

/forms · FormsPage.tsx · FormList.tsx
  1. P2Five identical filled buttons
    Every row ends in the same heavy navy "Open ↗", so nothing on the page stands out. The ↗ icon means "opens elsewhere", but the form opens in the same place. Make the whole row tappable, with a chevron at the end.
  2. P2"General" repeated on every row
    The category under each title is identical five times. Show it once as a group heading with a count, and use the second line for something useful: roughly how long the form takes, how many approvals, whether it needs a signature.
  3. P2An unexplained pencil icon
    The ✎ next to Open opens the builder, which is admin-only, yet it has the same weight as Open and no label on touch screens. Move "Edit in builder" into a ⋯ menu, or show it on hover for superusers only.
  4. P2"Open X?" before opening a form
    Clicking Open shows a confirmation dialog before the form opens. That's an extra step for the most common action in the app. Open the form directly. If it opens in a new tab, say so in the button's label.
    FormsPage.tsx:271–282
  5. P3The test form looks like a real one
    "ZZ TEST RUN" sits in the list with the same weight as live forms. Put it in its own "Testing · only you see these" group with a lock icon.
    superuserOnlyForms.ts
  6. P3Icon colours that mean nothing
    The icon tiles are purple, grey, red, amber and red in turn. Red reads as "error". Either use one tonal colour, or give each colour a meaning (the form's category) and show a legend.
    META_PALETTES in spConfig.ts

My Submissions

/submissions · MySubmissionsPage.tsx · SubmissionRow.tsx
  1. P1The card is titled with your own name
    On My Submissions, every card is headed "Aiman Rahman Bin Zainal", which is you, every time. The form name is pushed into a small chip. Lead with the form, then the reference number and the step it's waiting on.
    SubmissionRow.tsx
  2. P1Delete is as prominent as View
    The red-filled delete square sits right next to View at the same size, the easiest thing on the card to hit by mistake. Make it a text button ("Withdraw") and show an Undo bar for 6 seconds instead of a confirmation dialog. Try it in the mockup. The current dialog talks about "the SharePoint item … matrix child rows", and the success message says "Removed N managed files and N matrix rows". Replace both with "Submission deleted."
    SubmissionRow.tsx:159 · MySubmissionsPage.tsx:204–212, 376–379
  3. P2A "view" icon that isn't a button, inside a card that is
    The eye icon is decoration, not a button. The real delete button sits inside a clickable card, which confuses screen readers. Replace them with a row that has one chevron and a ⋯ menu.
    SubmissionRow.tsx:116, 188–202
  4. P2Seven names for a status, and ten filter controls
    "Pending", "In Review", "still moving", "Being reviewed", "Fully Approved" next to "Approved", and "Confirmed". The toolbar offers ten filters, including developer-only ones ("Profile", "Version"). Use three words: In review, Approved, Sent back. Show three chips, and put the rest behind the filter icon.
    StatusBadge.tsx:13–20 · Toolbar.tsx:408–433
  5. P2"Submitted by SharePoint App"
    This is an internal system name leaking onto the screen. Hide it when the submitter is the system account, or say "Submitted from a public link".
  6. P2The search hint is cut off
    "Search reference, form or ID.." (two dots) is cut off at phone width. Shorten it to "Search by form or ref no.".
  7. P2Three controls before the first result
    Search, a "Form type" dropdown and an "Advanced Search" button sit inside their own box above the list. Gmail's pattern: one pill search with a filter icon inside it, and chips below (Status, Form, Any time) that open small menus. Also, the "where is it now" question gets a 3-dot progress track instead of a lone "In Review" tag.

All Submissions (admin)

/admin/submissions · ApprovalDashboard.tsx

The screen most used for repeated admin work, and the one where a stuck load hurts most.

  1. P1It loads forever when a service is down
    With the app's own server unavailable, the page showed "Loading approvals..." for over 10 seconds and never changed: no timeout, no error, no retry, just a grey sentence in an empty box. Show placeholder rows straight away. After about 8 seconds, say it's taking longer than usual and offer Retry. If it fails, say what couldn't be reached.
    src/components/builder/ApprovalDashboard.tsx
  2. P2"Approvals" on a tab called "Submissions"
    The tab says All Submissions, the loading text says "approvals", and the browser tab says "Submissions — PMW HR Form". Use one word.
  3. P2A rich admin list with no bulk actions
    HR repeats the same decision across many rows. Copy Gmail: tick boxes that switch the toolbar to Approve / Remind / Reassign, and Shift-click to select a range. The working mockup above shows this.
  4. P2Two page titles, and a red "TEST" tag
    The page adds its own "Submissions" heading under the app's "Forms" heading, so there are two top-level headings that disagree. Test runs get a red tag, but red means "failed". It also uses plain browser dropdowns and the builder's colours inside the app. Show "Test run" in amber, use the same dropdowns as everywhere else, and hide the "Profile (developer-reference metadata)" column.
    ApprovalDashboard.tsx:3144, 3191, 3445, 3484
  5. P322 hard-coded colours in one file
    The most of any file: mostly #fff and #FCA5A5 written next to the shared colour settings, so a future theme change will miss them.
    ApprovalDashboard.tsx:3149, 3170–3272

Profile

/profile · ProfilePage.tsx
  1. P3The email address appears twice
    It's under the name and again in the Email row. Put the job title and department under the name instead, since that's what identifies a person at work.
  2. P3Grey capital labels with icons for four fields
    "@ EMAIL", "JOB TITLE", "DEPARTMENT" and "PHONE" each get a capital label, an icon and a divider line. Use flat rows with a round icon, the value as the main line and the label underneath.
  3. P2"These come from SharePoint group membership"
    This explains the system rather than the person's situation. Say "Ask HR if something here is wrong" and show roles as two pills under the name. The long descriptions belong in a tooltip.

    Tiny 7px capital-letter tags next to a tick say the same thing twice. Showing a role at all means you have it.
  5. P3Profile, Appearance and Privacy as three tabs
    A personal page reads better as one scrolling page with links, like the Google Account page, than as three tabs in a strip.
  6. P2Appearance is under Profile, but only admins can use it
    The background applies to the whole company, and only admins can change it, yet the tab shows for everyone under Profile. An employee opens a page where nothing works. Move it to Admin as "Branding".

Appearance

/profile/appearance · AppearancePage.tsx · BackgroundPicker.tsx
  1. P2The explanation reads like a design note
    "The flat canvas is the default and the one the rest of this design is built for; the gallery offers tinted gradients…" is written for designers. Replace it with "Plain is the default. Changes save straight away."

    "Currently applied: Editorial Sky" asks people to imagine what that looks like. Show a live preview at the top and round swatches below it (try it), with a tick on the selected one. No "Change background" button or extra dialog needed.

Learning hub

/learning · LearningMaterialsPage.tsx · LearningHeader.tsx · TopicCard / MaterialCard
  1. P1Four headers stacked before any content
    "Internal Portal" title, then the Job Portal/Learning tabs, then a "Learning Materials" bar showing the logo again with Refresh and Manage content, then a large "Learn at your own pace" banner. Content starts about 450px down a phone screen. Start with search and a "Continue" row. "Manage content" belongs in Admin, or in a ⋯ menu for admins.
    LearningHeader.tsx · LearningMaterialsPage.tsx

  5. P3An "All topics" pill on its own
    A one-item breadcrumb floats on the background. Only show the breadcrumb once you're inside a topic. Locked topics should look locked at a glance, with a round lock icon in place of a thumbnail.

When a form link fails

/form/:formId · DynamicFormPage.tsx

This is what someone sees after scanning a printed QR code when something goes wrong. I didn't open the form itself, as you asked.

  1. P0Placeholder text where the icons should be
    The code puts the letters "ERR" (44px) and "LOCK" (30px) where icons belong, and they ship to users. This is the screen someone lands on from an outdated QR code, and it looks unfinished. Use a round illustration and a plain-language headline, then two pill buttons: Try again and Go to my forms. Move the technical detail into one small line at the bottom.
    DynamicFormPage.tsx:2163, 2174 ("ERR") · 666 ("LOCK")
  2. P1The wrong message
    The server was down (502), but the page says "Form not found", which tells someone their QR code or link is broken. Tell "doesn't exist" (404), "can't reach" (5xx) and "closed" (unpublished) apart, and write each one for a person.
  3. P2"Server returned status 502:"
    A raw status code with a colon and nothing after it. Put the code in small type with the form name, so someone can quote it to HR.
  4. P2The tab says "Form: Form"
    A placeholder has slipped into the browser tab title. Before the form loads, use "Loading form · PMW HR"; on error, "Form unavailable · PMW HR".
  5. P3Thin border, heavy shadow
    A 0.8px border with a 42px blurred shadow (flagged by the scanner). Under the new direction there's no card at all; the message sits directly on the page.
    detector: gpt-thin-border-wide-shadow
